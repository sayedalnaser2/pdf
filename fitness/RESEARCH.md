## Endurance

### Running (5K–marathon)

**Training plan** (evidence R — Research-based): Heavy low-rep lifting (3 × 4–6) and plyometrics improve running economy more than light high-rep work. Single-leg strength, calves/tibialis and hamstring/adductor work keep hips, shins and Achilles resilient.

- Sport-specific work added to every lifting day: Jump power, Ankles & balance, Hamstrings, Adductors / hips
- Exercise pool: Box jump (step down); Broad jump; Lateral bound (skater jump); Pogo hops; Drop-landing & stick; Explosive step-up (low impact); Nordic hamstring curl; Hamstring curl machine; Slider / towel hamstring curl; Single-leg Romanian deadlift; Single-leg RDL (bodyweight); Back extension (hyper); Copenhagen plank; Dumbbell lateral lunge; Cossack squat; Adductor squeeze (pillow / ball); Adductor machine; Single-leg balance reach; Tibialis raise (back to wall); Single-leg calf raise; Banded ankle eversion; Single-leg hop & stick
- Preferred main lifts: Eccentric step-down (slow lowering); Step-ups (chair/box); Barbell back squat; Leg press; Goblet squat
- De-emphasised: Biceps, Triceps, Overhead push
- Heavy, low-rep lifting (3 × 4–6) with long rests, to build strength without adding fatigue
- Sport sessions per week: {"4":2,"5":2,"6":3} (by training days)
- Source: [Strength training and running economy — 2024 meta-analysis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11052887/)

**Rest** (evidence C — Coaching consensus)
- Heavy-lift rest on lifting days: 150 s
- 3–5 min VO₂max repeats: 2–4 min easy jog (≈1:1 work:rest, 3 min typical)
- 30/30 intervals: 30 s easy jog between reps
- Tempo / cruise intervals: 60–90 s jog
- Strides: 60–90 s walk-back (full recovery, form first)

### Cycling (road, MTB, indoor)

**Training plan** (evidence R — Research-based): Heavy half-squats and single-leg presses (3 × 4–10) improved cycling economy ~5% and Wingate peak power ~10% in trained cyclists. Back-extension and core work protect the lower back in the saddle.

- Sport-specific work added to every lifting day: Glutes, Ankles & balance, Hamstrings, Core
- Exercise pool: Nordic hamstring curl; Hamstring curl machine; Slider / towel hamstring curl; Single-leg Romanian deadlift; Single-leg RDL (bodyweight); Back extension (hyper); Single-leg balance reach; Tibialis raise (back to wall); Single-leg calf raise; Banded ankle eversion; Single-leg hop & stick
- Preferred main lifts: Half squat (heavy); Single-leg leg press; Barbell back squat; Isometric squat hold; Romanian deadlift; Back extension (hyper)
- De-emphasised: Biceps, Triceps, Overhead push, Calves
- Heavy, low-rep lifting (3 × 4–6) with long rests, to build strength without adding fatigue
- Sport sessions per week: {"4":2,"5":2,"6":3} (by training days)
- Source: [Heavy strength training improves cycling economy (RCT, trained cyclists)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25892654/)

**Rest** (evidence C — Coaching consensus)
- Heavy-lift rest on lifting days: 150 s
- VO₂max intervals (3–8 min): recovery ≈ equal to work (1:1)
- Threshold intervals: rest about half the work time (15 min on → 7–8 min off)
- Sweet spot: short recovery (≈5 min after 15–20 min blocks) to keep the stimulus going
- Rule of thumb: if power drops >5% below target, lengthen the rest

## Water

### Swimming

**Training plan** (evidence C — Coaching consensus): Lat-focused pulling (pulldowns, straight-arm pulldowns) supports the stroke, rear-delt/rotator-cuff work protects swimmers’ shoulders, and hollow-body/flutter core work builds streamline stability.

- Sport-specific work added to every lifting day: Vertical pull, Shoulder health, Upper-body power, Core
- Exercise pool: Medicine-ball slam; Medicine-ball chest pass (wall); Overhead medicine-ball throw; Explosive push-up (hands leave floor); Band explosive chest press; Band external rotation; Side-lying dumbbell external rotation; Face pull (cable); Band face pull; Scapular push-up; Wall slides; Prone Y-T-W raises; Serratus punch (band); Straight-arm pulldown (cable); Straight-arm band pulldown; Hollow body hold; Flutter kicks

- De-emphasised: Calves, Biceps
- Sport sessions per week: {"4":2,"5":2,"6":3} (by training days)
- Source: [Dry-land training for swimmers — systematic review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8431432/)

**Rest** (evidence C — Coaching consensus)
- Heavy-lift rest on lifting days: 100 s
- Aerobic sets: 20–30 s rest on short repeats, 30–60 s on 200s
- Threshold 100s: 20–40 s rest
- Sprints (10–50 m): full recovery, work:rest ≈ 1:6 or more
- Rest = same time as the swim (1:1) trains lactate tolerance; much longer shifts the set to aerobic

### Powerlifting

**Training plan** (evidence R — Research-based): The three competition lifts come first (squat/bench/deadlift), with pause/box/deficit variations to attack sticking points, and triceps, rows, glutes and core as the accessories that raise the big three.

- Sport-specific work added to every lifting day: Triceps, Row, Glutes, Core
- Exercise pool: Pendlay row; Skull crusher; Close-grip bench press
- Preferred main lifts: Barbell back squat; Pause squat; Box squat; Conventional deadlift; Deficit deadlift; Good morning; Barbell bench press; Pause bench press; Close-grip bench press; Pendlay row; Barbell row; Skull crusher
- De-emphasised: Calves, Rear delts, Biceps
- No separate sport session: the lifts themselves are the sport

**Rest** (evidence R — Research-based)
- Heavy-lift rest on lifting days: 210 s (upper-body ×0.75)
- Squat & deadlift: 3–5 min between heavy sets (3–5 min beat 1 min for absolute strength)
- Bench press & presses: 2–3 min is enough
- Accessories: 90–120 s
- Speed sets: 90–120 s — don’t let bar speed drop
